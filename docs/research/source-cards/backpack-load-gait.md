# Source Card: Backpack Load Gait

## Metadata

Type: biomechanics research topic.

Reliability: medium to high.

Relevance: high.

## What it says

Carrying a rear load changes posture and walking. The most useful HLS effects are forward torso lean, shorter steps, reduced arm freedom, and increased upper body stiffness.

## Useful HLS Facts

- Rear load increases forward torso lean.
- Heavy load reduces step length.
- Heavy load can reduce arm freedom.
- Unstable load can add delayed sway.

## Candidate HLS Rules

- Rear load increases torso pitch.
- Higher load reduces step length.
- Higher load increases spine stiffness.
- Unstable load adds secondary sway.

## Uncertainty

Research values differ by carried mass, subject age, speed, and pack design.
